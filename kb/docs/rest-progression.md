# Rest between sets and progression

## Rest between sets
A 2024 meta-analysis of nine studies compared shorter rests (60 seconds or less) with longer ones (over 60 seconds) when all other training was matched. Both produced muscle growth. The authors report a small benefit for resting longer than 60 seconds, possibly because longer rests let people complete more total work, but the uncertainty ranges include no difference. They found no appreciable difference once rests went beyond about 90 seconds. The 2009 ACSM position stand suggests 1–2 minutes between sets for muscle growth, and longer rests (3–5 minutes) for heavy strength work. These are typical ranges from research, not a rule for any one person.

## Progression
For muscle to keep adapting, the training stress has to increase over time (progressive overload). The ACSM statement gives one concrete rule: when you can do one or two reps more than your target at a given load, increase the load by about 2–10%. It also recommends varying volume and intensity over time and notes that exercises should be ordered so the large, demanding movements come first, while you are fresh.

Progression can also mean adding a rep or a set before adding weight, and the evidence on weekly sets (see the volume document) shows returns shrink as sets pile up, so more is not always better. The sources do not say how fast any one person should progress.

## Limits of this evidence
The rest-interval analysis had few studies, mostly in untrained or short-term settings, and its authors report substantial differences between studies. The ACSM position stand is from 2009, and only its abstract was read for this document.
