# Protein and energy: typical ranges

## Protein
A typical protein range for people who lift weights is 1.6–2.2 grams per kilogram of total body weight per day. The range applies when body weight is known: multiply it by body weight in kilograms. A larger meta-analysis of resistance training in healthy adults found that extra protein stopped adding muscle gain at about 1.6 g/kg/day, with a wide uncertainty band (1.03–2.20 g/kg/day) and a result that was not statistically significant. Its authors suggest about 2.2 g/kg/day as a practical upper estimate. A review for natural bodybuilders also gives 1.6–2.2, and an international sports-nutrition position stand gives 1.4–2.0 as enough for most exercising people. These are averages from research, not a personal prescription.

Spreading protein across the day is common advice, but the sources differ on the amount per meal: one gives 0.40–0.55 g/kg per meal over 3–6 meals, another about 0.25 g/kg or 20–40 g per dose every 3–4 hours. This plan does not set a per-meal number.

Higher intakes (2.3–3.1 g/kg/day) are discussed for lean, resistance-trained people during an energy deficit. The sources state the unit differently (body weight versus lean body mass), so this plan does not prescribe them.

## Energy
Reviews written for natural bodybuilders describe a slight surplus of about 10–20% above maintenance during a gaining phase, with a weight gain of about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week for novice and intermediate lifters; more experienced lifters should aim for the lower end or be more conservative. For losing weight, a rate of about 0.5–1% of body weight per week is described for muscle retention. These reviews address bodybuilders, not the general public; applying them to recreational lifters is an extrapolation, so treat them as typical ranges, not targets.

This app's own calorie limits are separate and stricter than these ranges: a deficit never goes below the person's estimated resting needs and never exceeds 20% of estimated daily needs, and a surplus never exceeds 10%. Those limits come from the app's design, not from these papers.

## Medical conditions
If you have kidney disease or any other medical condition, talk to a doctor or dietitian before changing your protein or calorie intake. This is not medical advice.
