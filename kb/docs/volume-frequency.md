# Weekly volume and per-session volume: typical ranges

## What "volume" means here
Volume is the number of hard sets a muscle group gets in a week. A set counts as 1 for a muscle that is the main mover in the exercise and 0.5 for a muscle that helps. A meta-regression of 67 studies (2,058 people, mostly young adults trained for about 10 weeks) found this "fractional" counting fit hypertrophy better than counting every set equally or counting only direct sets. This app applies the same idea to the exercise library's primary and secondary muscle lists, which were not written with that definition, so the match is approximate.

## Weekly volume
In that analysis, about 4 fractional sets per muscle group per week was the lowest amount linked to detectable muscle growth on average. It is a typical minimum from research, not a prediction for any one person and not a target. Results improve with more sets, but with diminishing returns: the fitted curve rises as a square root, and the average added growth was about 0.24% per extra weekly set (uncertainty about 0.15–0.33%). The analysis grouped volume into bands (5–10, 11–18, 19–29, 30–42 and 43 or more weekly sets), and each higher band gave less extra growth per set than the one before. The studies' median was about 10.5 weekly sets for growth outcomes.

## Volume in one session
A second analysis, a preprint that is not peer reviewed, looked at sets within a single session. It put the point where extra sets stopped showing clear extra growth at about 11 fractional sets per muscle group per session. Its authors say this should be read cautiously because few studies used very high per-session volumes. When a muscle group needs more than that in a week, the plan spreads the sets over more sessions.

## Limits of this evidence
Both analyses are preprints, and the journal version of the first may report different numbers. The participants were mostly young adults over about 10 weeks, so older people, long programmes and people returning from a break are not well covered. Volume is one input among others, such as effort, exercise choice, sleep and food.

## Training frequency (sessions per muscle group per week)
For muscle size, the same analysis found that training a muscle group more often per week had a small effect at most. The best-fitting model gave a 91.3% probability that more sessions helped, but the uncertainty range was compatible with no effect, and the analyses of direct sets alone showed none. Its authors conclude that any independent effect of extra frequency is small and not consistently identifiable. In practice this means the weekly number of sets matters more than how they are split across days, as long as no single session goes past the per-session guideline above. The studies averaged about 13 weekly fractional sets, so the finding is least certain for very high or very low volumes. Frequency mattered more for strength than for size, which is not this app's main goal.
