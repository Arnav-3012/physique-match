# Rep ranges and how close to failure

## Rep ranges
The traditional advice is 8–12 reps per set at about 60–80% of the heaviest load you can lift once (1RM). A 2021 review tested that idea against the research and found it is not well supported. Studies comparing heavy and light loads found similar muscle growth across a wide range of loads, from roughly 30% of 1RM upward, and the finding did not depend on age or training status. The authors conclude there is no single ideal "hypertrophy zone".

The review also points out trade-offs. Heavy loads needed more sets to produce the same growth as moderate loads. Very light loads need many more reps per set, which takes longer. For that reason moderate loads are described as the most time-efficient choice. A 2009 position stand from the American College of Sports Medicine (older than these analyses) recommends loads of 8–12 reps for beginners, and for muscle growth a mix of loads with most work in the 6–12 rep range. Few of the studies in the review were done in women, so the evidence is thinner for them.

## How close to failure
"Failure" means the set ends because you cannot complete another rep with good form. A 2023 meta-analysis of 15 studies compared sets taken to failure with sets stopped earlier. Taking sets to failure showed a trivial advantage that was borderline (effect size 0.19, 95% interval 0.00 to 0.37). When only studies using true momentary failure were counted, there was no clear difference (0.12, interval −0.13 to 0.37). The authors found no evidence that training to failure builds more muscle than stopping short of it.

The same paper notes a limit: in the studies that stopped short of failure, it is hard to know how close to failure people really got, so it is difficult to say how far from failure is far enough. It also suggests that lighter loads may call for ending sets closer to failure than heavy loads do. This app does not give a number of reps to leave in reserve, because the sections of the paper that discuss practical use were not checked.

## Limits of this evidence
Most studies were short and used mostly untrained adults. The ACSM statement is from 2009 and follows older research. Effort, exercise choice and total weekly sets all interact, so a rep range alone does not decide results.
